E. coli Aerobic Fermentation

20 L bubble column — high growth, O₂ demand

A lab-scale bubble column (20 L) cultivating E. coli at 37°C with vigorous aeration. The fast-growing culture (μ_max = 0.7 h⁻¹) rapidly consumes dissolved oxygen, driving kLa-limited mass transfer. As biomass accumulates, the oxygen uptake rate exceeds the transfer capacity, creating a classic O₂-limited fermentation profile. CO₂ accumulates from respiration.

CHO Cell Bioproduction

250 L stirred tank — slow growth, controlled O₂

A pilot-scale stirred-tank bioreactor (250 L) for CHO cell culture at 37°C. These mammalian cells grow slowly (μ_max = 0.04 h⁻¹) but require precise dissolved oxygen control for protein production. The impeller provides additional mixing, supplementing gas sparging. Over 7 days, biomass increases steadily while DO is maintained near saturation by the high kLa from combined sparging and agitation.

Yeast Aerobic Cultivation

10,000 L airlift — scale-up, mass transfer limited

An industrial-scale airlift reactor (10,000 L) cultivating Saccharomyces cerevisiae at 30°C. Despite high absolute gas flow (50 L/min), the large volume results in low superficial gas velocity and limited mass transfer. The yeast’s moderate growth rate (μ_max = 0.35 h⁻¹) and high O₂ demand per biomass quickly deplete dissolved oxygen, demonstrating the classic scale-up challenge where kLa fails to meet biological demand.
