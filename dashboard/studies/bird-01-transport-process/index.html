Claim

Can the gas–liquid transport physics be extracted into a shared module and exposed as a biomass-as-input BiRDTransportProcess that emits only the transport contribution, while the standalone BiRDReactorProcess (now transport + an extracted MonodCellProcess) keeps its pre-refactor behavior?

The current BiRDReactorProcess owns biomass internally through a Monod growth ODE. When it is coupled to a cell engine that *also* owns biomass — a dFBA model or the v2ecoli whole-cell model — the two ownerships collide. Today that is worked around with a `bird_disable_internal_biomass` flag that runs the process with half of itself switched off, which is interface-dishonest and breaks the substitutability symmetry the cell-side interface contract is meant to guarantee. The physics that genuinely belongs to the vessel is gas–liquid mass transfer: the volumetric transfer coefficient kLa (from Higbie penetration theory), the temperature-dependent Henry's-law saturation concentration C*, and Wilke–Chang liquid diffusivity. For a 0D well-mixed reactor the transport contribution to a dissolved species is kLa·(C*−C): positive uptake for O2, symmetric stripping for CO2, and exactly zero for glucose, which has no gas phase. The fix is a single-responsibility split. Transport physics moves into a shared module; the biomass/consumption term is factored into a small MonodCellProcess that conforms to the cell-side contract; and a new BiRDTransportProcess reads biomass, substrate, and dissolved-gas concentrations as inputs and emits only the transport deltas. Standalone BiRDReactorProcess becomes MonodCellProcess + transport recomposed, so its behavior is preserved exactly. Because process-bigraph aggregates updates, the cell's exchange deltas and the reactor's transport deltas land at the same dissolved-species stores and sum cleanly. This matters because it unlocks feedback a single-process reactor cannot express: once biomass is external, a dense culture can draw dissolved O2 below saturation and the reactor state responds to the cell — the bidirectional coupling that Phases 2–4 test, and the reactor-physics dependency that v2ecoli's mbp-03 study is blocked on.

Test

Extract the transport physics into a shared module, factor the internal Monod term into a MonodCellProcess, and build BiRDTransportProcess; then measure emitted deltas, contract conformance, and the standalone reactor's trajectories to determine that responsibilities are cleanly split and standalone behavior is preserved.

Result

A single-responsibility split lets the reactor own only kLa·(C*−C) transport while the cell engine owns biomass and exchange; under process-bigraph update aggregation the two sets of deltas merge at the shared dissolved-species stores without either process owning the other's state.
